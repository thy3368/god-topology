# China Macro Causal Chain Templates

Use these templates to turn common China macro claims into falsifiable document chains. Adapt the wording to the user's exact question; do not force a template when the facts point elsewhere.

## 房地产收储

macro_claim:
Local government or SOE-platform inventory purchases are reducing developer inventory, improving developer cash flow, and stabilizing local housing markets.

policy_tool:
Central relending facility, local acquisition plan, affordable housing conversion policy, SOE platform purchase mandate, bank credit line.

document chain:
- Level 1: central or local policy notice supporting inventory acquisition.
- Level 2: relending quota, local implementation plan, eligible project list, bank facility allocation.
- Level 3: acquisition bidding notice, winning bid, purchase agreement, bank loan contract, housing conversion contract.
- Level 4: payment record, deed transfer, delivery acceptance, housing-use conversion approval, bank disbursement.
- Level 5: developer cash receipts, inventory reduction, impairment reversal or recognition, SOE platform assets/liabilities, local fiscal impact.
- Level 6: local inventory months, new-home prices, second-hand prices, sales area, completed-home inventory, developer funding sources.

where to verify:
- Policy and implementation plans: People's Bank of China, Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, city housing bureau, city government website.
- Project lists and acquisition notices: city housing bureau, state-owned assets supervision commission, public resource trading center.
- Bidding and winning-bid documents: China Government Procurement Network, provincial/city government procurement network, provincial/city public resource trading center.
- Property transfer and delivery: local real estate registration center is often non-public; use public announcements, SOE disclosures, listed developer announcements, and local housing bureau notices as public proxies.
- Developer financial impact: Shanghai/Shenzhen/Hong Kong stock exchange filings, company interim/annual reports, bond issuer reports.
- Local macro confirmation: National Bureau of Statistics, local statistics bureau, housing bureau inventory data, CRIC/China Index Academy if official inventory data is missing.

key distinctions:
- Announced收储规模 is not actual purchase.
- Loan quota is not disbursement.
- Purchase contract is not payment.
- Inventory transferred to SOE is not necessarily absorbed end demand.

contradictions:
Low bid coverage, delayed payment, homes bought at deep discount, conversion creates SOE leverage stress, local inventory months do not fall, developer cash flow does not improve.

expected lag:
1-3 months from policy to project list; 3-9 months to contracts/payments; 6-12 months to local inventory and price evidence.

## 专项债 / 基建

macro_claim:
Special bond issuance is expanding infrastructure demand rather than refinancing old obligations or filling fiscal gaps.

policy_tool:
Local government special bond quota, project capital injection, matching bank loans, central transfer payment, policy bank support.

document chain:
- Level 1: State Council, MOF, NDRC, or local policy on special bond usage.
- Level 2: annual quota, issuance plan, bond prospectus, project list, fund-use table.
- Level 3: project approval, EIA approval, bidding notice, winning bid, construction contract, supervision contract.
- Level 4: fund allocation, contractor payment, invoice, construction progress report, completion acceptance.
- Level 5: contractor revenue/cash receipts, local fiscal expenditure, fixed-asset investment by sector, LGFV balance sheet.
- Level 6: infrastructure FAI, cement/asphalt/steel demand, excavator hours, construction PMI, fiscal expenditure data.

where to verify:
- Quota and policy: Ministry of Finance, provincial finance departments, NDRC, local development and reform commissions.
- Bond prospectus and fund-use table: ChinaBond, Shanghai Clearing House, local government bond information disclosure portals.
- Project approvals and EIA: local development and reform commission, ecology and environment bureau.
- Bidding and construction contracts: provincial/city public resource trading center, China Government Procurement Network.
- Contractor financial impact: stock exchange filings, listed company announcements, bond issuer periodic reports.
- Macro confirmation: National Bureau of Statistics, local statistics bureau, Ministry of Finance fiscal expenditure data, industry association data for cement/steel/machinery.

key distinctions:
- Bond issuance is not project spending.
- Project inclusion is not work-start.
- Infrastructure nominal investment may reflect land, fees, or price rather than physical work.
- Refinancing or arrears repayment is not new demand.

contradictions:
High share used for debt repayment, weak project bidding, slow fiscal expenditure, contractor receivables rising, commodity demand not responding.

expected lag:
1-2 months from issuance to allocation; 2-6 months to contractor payments and physical activity; 3-9 months to macro FAI confirmation.

## 化债 / 地方财政

macro_claim:
Debt resolution is reducing local government financing stress and restoring spending capacity.

policy_tool:
Debt-swap bonds, special refinancing bonds, fiscal transfers, LGFV restructuring, bank loan extension, interest-rate reduction.

document chain:
- Level 1: debt-resolution policy statement or local fiscal work plan.
- Level 2: refinancing bond quota, bond issuance documents, budget adjustment, bank restructuring framework.
- Level 3: swap agreement, bank extension agreement, LGFV debt replacement notice, creditor meeting documents.
- Level 4: old debt repayment, new bond proceeds received, interest payment records, arrears repayment to suppliers.
- Level 5: local fiscal interest burden, LGFV maturity profile, supplier receivables, bank loan classification, fiscal expenditure recovery.
- Level 6: local fiscal expenditure, infrastructure spending, LGFV credit spreads, arrears indicators, regional bank asset quality.

where to verify:
- Policy and quota: Ministry of Finance, provincial finance departments, local budget adjustment reports.
- Bond issuance: ChinaBond, Shanghai Clearing House, local government bond prospectuses.
- LGFV debt profile: exchange bond filings, ChinaBond issuer reports, rating agency follow-up reports.
- Bank restructuring details: often non-public/internal; use listed bank disclosures, bond rating reports, and LGFV announcements as public proxies.
- Arrears and supplier impact: listed supplier receivables, government procurement payment notices, court enforcement records when relevant.
- Macro confirmation: local fiscal monthly data, Ministry of Finance data, LGFV credit spreads, regional bank asset-quality disclosures.

key distinctions:
- Replacing hidden debt lowers rollover pressure but may not create new demand.
- Lower interest burden is not equivalent to fiscal expansion.
- Arrears repayment improves supplier cash flow but may not increase current production.

contradictions:
New borrowing restrictions offset relief, fiscal expenditure remains weak, LGFV spreads stay high, supplier receivables keep rising, land sales continue falling.

expected lag:
1-3 months to issuance and swaps; 3-6 months to cash-flow relief; 6-12 months to fiscal spending recovery if it occurs.

## 新质生产力 / 先进制造

macro_claim:
New productive forces policy is raising productivity and sustainable profit growth rather than only expanding capacity.

policy_tool:
Industrial subsidy, tax credit, equipment renewal policy, government guidance fund, procurement, credit support, R&D grant.

document chain:
- Level 1: central or local industrial policy, catalog, action plan.
- Level 2: subsidy budget, fund setup, project list, tax incentive catalog, equipment renewal quota.
- Level 3: grant award, procurement contract, equipment order, factory expansion approval, bank loan contract.
- Level 4: subsidy payment, equipment delivery, capacity commissioning, patent acceptance, product certification.
- Level 5: unit cost decline, capacity utilization, ASP, gross margin, ROIC, operating cash flow, inventory, receivables, export profitability.
- Level 6: manufacturing productivity, industrial profit, capacity utilization, PPI, export volume/value split, sector capex.

where to verify:
- Policy catalogues and project lists: MIIT, NDRC, Ministry of Science and Technology, local industry and information technology bureaus.
- Subsidy budgets and award lists: local finance departments, local industry bureaus, government fund websites.
- Procurement and equipment orders: China Government Procurement Network, public resource trading centers, listed company announcements.
- Certification and patents: CNIPA, SAMR, sector regulator certification platforms.
- Financial impact: stock exchange filings, company annual/interim reports, bond issuer reports.
- Macro confirmation: National Bureau of Statistics industrial data, customs data, industry association capacity/utilization data, PPI by sector.

key distinctions:
- Capacity expansion is not productivity improvement.
- Subsidized sales can inflate revenue while reducing profitability.
- Patent count or policy recognition is not commercial adoption.
- Nominal output growth may be price, inventory, or channel stuffing.

contradictions:
Falling ASP, rising inventories, weak cash conversion, lower ROIC, sector-wide overcapacity, export volume growth with profit compression.

expected lag:
3-9 months to project/equipment execution; 6-18 months to financial impact; 12-36 months to productivity confirmation.

## 社融 / 信用周期

macro_claim:
Stronger social financing means broad credit transmission into real economic activity.

policy_tool:
Reserve requirement change, policy bank lending, credit guidance, mortgage easing, bill financing, government bond issuance, relending facility.

document chain:
- Level 1: monetary or credit policy statement.
- Level 2: facility quota, bank credit target, government bond issuance, mortgage policy, relending allocation.
- Level 3: loan contracts, mortgage approvals, project-loan approvals, corporate borrowing disclosures.
- Level 4: loan disbursement, supplier payment, capex payment, home transaction settlement.
- Level 5: borrower capex, working capital use, sales growth, inventory, interest expense, bank loan mix and asset quality.
- Level 6: private credit demand, M1/M2, FAI, retail sales, property sales, industrial output, inflation, employment.

where to verify:
- Policy and aggregate credit: People's Bank of China, NFRA, NBS, Ministry of Finance for government financing.
- Government bond financing: ChinaBond, Shanghai Clearing House, MOF/local finance departments.
- Corporate borrowing: stock exchange announcements, bond prospectuses, listed company reports.
- Mortgage and property transaction proxies: city housing bureaus, real estate transaction centers where public, listed bank reports.
- Bank loan mix and asset quality: listed bank interim/annual reports, PBOC financial statistics reports.
- Macro confirmation: PBOC TSF/M1/M2 data, NBS FAI/retail/property data, local statistics bureaus.

key distinctions:
- Government bond financing can lift TSF without private credit appetite.
- Bill financing and short-term loans may indicate weak demand.
- Loan approval is not disbursement or spending.
- Credit quantity without velocity can be balance-sheet repair.

contradictions:
M1 weak, deposits rising, private borrowing weak, bill financing high, corporate capex low, household deleveraging continues.

expected lag:
1-3 months from policy to credit quantity; 3-6 months to spending; 6-12 months to income/inflation confirmation.

## 出口 / 海关单据

macro_claim:
Export growth reflects real external demand rather than price cuts, front-loading, transshipment, subsidies, or inventory cycles.

policy_tool:
Trade facilitation, export tax rebate, industrial subsidy, FX effects, customs policy, bilateral demand shock.

document chain:
- Level 1: trade policy, customs guidance, rebate policy, foreign demand policy.
- Level 2: rebate budget, sector support list, port facilitation plan, export-credit facility.
- Level 3: export order, shipping contract, letter of credit, insurance policy, customer contract.
- Level 4: customs declaration, bill of lading, delivery confirmation, export rebate payment, foreign payment receipt.
- Level 5: exporter revenue, ASP, gross margin, receivables, inventory, FX gain/loss, cash conversion.
- Level 6: customs value/volume, destination mix, global import data, freight rates, competitor data, sector profit.

where to verify:
- Trade policy and rebates: Ministry of Commerce, General Administration of Customs, State Taxation Administration.
- Customs value/volume: General Administration of Customs, UN Comtrade, destination-country customs/statistics agencies.
- Shipping evidence: bill of lading and customer contracts are often non-public/internal; use port throughput, freight indexes, company disclosures, and customs data as public proxies.
- Export credit and insurance: Sinosure disclosures, policy bank or listed bank reports when available.
- Company financial impact: stock exchange filings, company reports, bond issuer reports.
- Price/volume and foreign demand: customs unit values, destination import data, freight rates, sector association data.

key distinctions:
- Export value growth is not necessarily volume growth.
- Volume growth with falling ASP may be price competition.
- Shipment is not final demand if foreign inventories rise.
- Re-routing via third countries can distort destination data.

contradictions:
Falling unit prices, margin compression, rising receivables, weak destination-country imports, freight anomalies, inventory accumulation abroad.

expected lag:
0-2 months from order/shipment to customs data; 1-3 months to company financials; 3-6 months to foreign inventory/demand validation.

## 消费刺激 / 补贴

macro_claim:
Consumption stimulus is creating incremental household demand rather than pulling forward purchases or shifting channels.

policy_tool:
Consumer subsidy, trade-in program, coupon, tax reduction, appliance/auto purchase support, local voucher.

document chain:
- Level 1: central or local consumption policy.
- Level 2: subsidy budget, eligible product list, merchant list, implementation rules.
- Level 3: merchant participation contract, platform agreement, subsidy application records.
- Level 4: subsidy payment, consumer invoice, delivery record, trade-in recycling record, settlement data.
- Level 5: retailer same-store sales, manufacturer shipments, channel inventory, gross margin, subsidy receivables, household finance data.
- Level 6: retail sales by category, penetration, replacement cycle, CPI/PPI, household income and confidence.

where to verify:
- Policy and budgets: Ministry of Commerce, NDRC, Ministry of Finance, local commerce bureaus, local finance departments.
- Eligible lists and merchant participation: local commerce bureau, government service platforms, program implementation notices.
- Subsidy settlement: some records are non-public/internal; use local public notices, audit reports, platform disclosures, and listed company subsidy receivables as public proxies.
- Sales and channel impact: listed retailer/manufacturer filings, industry association data, channel inventory surveys.
- Macro confirmation: NBS retail sales by category, local statistics bureaus, CPI/PPI, household income and confidence indicators.

key distinctions:
- Gross transaction value is not incremental demand.
- Subsidy usage can pull future purchases into the current period.
- Manufacturer shipment is not end-user sell-through.
- Nominal sales can rise while margins fall.

contradictions:
Post-subsidy sales slump, channel inventory build, subsidy receivables rising, weak household income, low repeat purchases, margin dilution.

expected lag:
Immediate usage data; 1-3 months to retailer/manufacturer financials; 3-9 months to check pull-forward and category-level retail confirmation.
