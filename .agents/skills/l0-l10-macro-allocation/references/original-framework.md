## Purpose

The framework converts China macro, policy, industry, regional, real estate, and personal decision questions into a layered judgment system. The goal is not to retell news, but to locate the binding layer that controls the decision and then test whether lower layers confirm or contradict it.

The central question is:

```text
Which layer is actually binding the outcome, and what evidence would prove that the binding layer has changed?
```

## Priority Order

Use this hierarchy:

```text
L0 > L6/L8 > L9 > L10 > L7 > story machine
```

Interpretation:

- L0 dominates because structural constraints define the possible set.
- L6/L8 matter because funding flows, balance sheets, industrial orders, and physical execution show whether policy is becoming reality.
- L9 matters because crack signals reveal stress, misallocation, enforcement risk, and hidden constraints.
- L10 is a slow-cycle reference, useful for long historical rhythm and regional path dependence.
- L7 matters only after the evidence stack is clear because prices can overshoot, lag, or already discount the conclusion.
- Story machine is only a source of topics, sentiment, and noise. It is never final evidence.

## Layer Map

### L0: Base Regime

Hard constraints and base conditions:

- demographics and household formation
- public and private balance sheets
- debt maturity structure
- fiscal capacity and land-finance dependence
- urbanization stage
- external demand and geopolitical constraint
- productivity and industrial upgrading
- employment and income distribution
- political economy incentives
- resource, energy, and geography constraints

Ask:

- Is this a cyclical problem or structural problem?
- Can policy reverse it, or only reallocate losses and smooth the path?
- Which actors must absorb the cost?
- What is the slow variable that cannot be faked?

### L1: Central Policy Intent And Tools

Official intent and concrete instruments:

- State Council, Politburo, Central Financial and Economic Affairs Commission
- PBOC, MOF, NDRC, MHRUD, MIIT, NFRA, CSRC
- fiscal quotas, special bonds, debt swaps, relending, reserve requirements
- procurement, subsidies, tax policy, regulatory priorities
- official plans, implementation opinions, budget documents

Ask:

- What is the exact policy objective?
- Which tool is being used?
- Who receives money, credit, orders, quota, or regulatory protection?
- Is the tool strong enough relative to L0?

### L6: Fiscal, Credit, And Money Flow

Implementation through money:

- credit growth, loan mix, PBOC facility usage
- fiscal revenue, tax revenue, non-tax revenue
- land transfer income
- transfer payments
- special bond issuance and use of proceeds
- debt replacement and refinancing
- LGFV bond documents and maturity walls
- arrears, procurement payment delays, salary pressure

Ask:

- Is money actually moving, or only promised?
- Is new money financing new demand or rolling old liabilities?
- Who is the payer of last resort?
- Does the cash flow match the policy narrative?

### L8: Industry And Corporate Execution

Implementation through companies and supply chains:

- orders, backlog, capacity utilization
- capex, depreciation, inventory
- gross margin, receivables, cash conversion
- export volume, price, customs data
- production schedules, lead times
- procurement awards and customer concentration
- listed company filings and earnings calls

Ask:

- Is there real demand or only thematic speculation?
- Which financial line changes first?
- Is the change large enough relative to company scale?
- Is the market misclassifying the company or industry?

### L9: Crack Signals

Stress signals that reveal contradiction between official story and implementation:

- CCDI investigation clusters
- audit-office findings
- finance bureau budget stress
- hidden debt disclosure and debt-swap language
- court enforcement and default records
- public resource trading cancellations or abnormal awards
- delayed payments to contractors or suppliers
- wage arrears and social-security payment stress
- local SOE, LGFV, bank, or developer balance-sheet strain
- unusual personnel changes in finance, state assets, land, housing, or project systems

Ask:

- Is the signal isolated, clustered, or systemic?
- Which balance sheet is cracking?
- Does it contradict the official story, or merely show cleanup under the same policy direction?
- What primary sources confirm it?

### L10: Long-Cycle Reference

Use as a slow background lens:

- long historical cycles
- regional rise and decline
- institutional path dependence
- migration and geography
- debt-cycle analogies
- cultural and administrative continuity
- Shao Yong / long-cycle style framing when requested by the user

Boundaries:

- L10 may frame multi-year or generational direction.
- L10 cannot decide short-term trading, individual stocks, precise dates, or policy timing.
- L10 cannot override observed L6/L8 evidence.

### L7: Market Pricing And Narrative

Market layer:

- prices, spreads, valuations
- equity sector rotation
- real estate price expectations
- bond yields and credit spreads
- positioning and liquidity
- consensus narratives

Ask:

- Is the evidence already priced?
- Is the market trading L1 slogans, L6/L8 proof, or L9 stress?
- Does valuation leave margin of safety?

### Story Machine

Story-machine inputs include media, experts, social platforms, slogans, and rumors.

Allowed uses:

- identify what people are talking about
- detect sentiment extremes
- discover topics for evidence checks
- compare consensus with primary evidence

Forbidden uses:

- proving policy success
- proving default risk
- proving demand
- proving asset undervaluation
- making final allocation decisions

## Analysis Flow

Use this sequence:

```text
1. Define the decision question.
2. Diagnose L0 constraints.
3. Identify L1 policy intent and tool.
4. Test L6 money flow and L8 corporate execution.
5. Search for L9 crack signals and cross-verify them.
6. Add L10 long-cycle frame only if helpful.
7. Compare with L7 pricing and story consensus.
8. State operational posture, confidence, validation, and invalidation.
```

## Evidence Families

Primary-source families:

- CCDI and local discipline inspection commissions
- National Audit Office and local audit offices
- MOF, local finance bureaus, budget reports, final accounts
- PBOC, NFRA, CSRC, NDRC, MHRUD, MIIT, customs, NBS
- ChinaBond, Shanghai Clearing House, exchange bond disclosures
- stock exchange filings and company annual/interim/quarterly reports
- public resource trading centers and government procurement sites
- court enforcement systems and bankruptcy notices
- local statistical yearbooks and official statistical bulletins

When facts may have changed, verify the latest official or primary source. Do not treat any cached number, official, quota, policy, city ranking, price, yield, or market cap as current without checking.

## Crack Signal Rules

L9 conclusions require source discipline.

Classification:

- `single-point`: one case, one person, one document, or one city.
- `clustered`: multiple cases in the same region, sector, agency chain, project type, or time window.
- `systemic`: multiple primary-source families show consistent stress across balance sheets, fiscal data, personnel discipline, arrears, market pricing, and project execution.

Minimum bar:

- To mention a crack signal: one credible source is enough if labeled clearly.
- To call it `clustered`: use multiple cases or documents.
- To call it `systemic`: use at least two primary-source families, preferably more.

Examples of useful pairings:

- CCDI investigation cluster + audit finding
- fiscal final accounts + LGFV bond prospectus
- procurement arrears + court enforcement records
- land-sale decline + local fiscal stress disclosure
- company receivables deterioration + government procurement payment delays

## Asset Allocation Mapping

Convert the layer stack into posture:

- L0 supportive, L6/L8 improving, L9 quiet, L7 cheap: constructive.
- L0 pressured, L1 supportive, L6/L8 absent, L9 noisy, L7 expensive: avoid or wait.
- L1 strong but L6/L8 weak: policy-watch, not allocation confirmation.
- L6/L8 strong but L7 already euphoric: quality may be real, but return/risk is lower.
- L9 systemic stress: reduce exposure, demand higher yield, or avoid levered balance sheets.

Use action words:

- avoid
- observe
- small test
- hold
- add after validation
- reduce
- hedge
- wait for documents
- no conclusion

## Regional Risk Mapping

For a province or city, inspect:

- population inflow or outflow
- industry mix and private-sector vitality
- fiscal revenue quality
- land-finance dependence
- transfer-payment dependence
- debt maturity and LGFV refinancing
- bank and local SOE exposure
- real estate inventory and price-volume trend
- L9 signals around finance, land, housing, state assets, transport, construction, and platforms

Do not judge a city only by GDP rank or housing price level.

## Real Estate Mapping

For buying property or judging real estate policy:

- L0: demographics, household income, urban tier, inventory, migration, balance-sheet capacity.
- L1: purchase restrictions, mortgage policy, tax policy, destocking, urban village renovation, social housing.
- L6: mortgage credit, local fiscal support, special bond use, land revenue, acquisition funding.
- L8: developer cash receipts, completions, inventories, sales conversion, property-management quality.
- L9: unfinished projects, arrears, developer lawsuits, local fiscal stress, discipline inspection in land and housing systems.
- L7: price, rent yield, vacancy, transaction volume, buyer sentiment.

Operational conclusion should distinguish:

- owner-occupied need vs investment demand
- core city/core district vs weak suburb
- new house vs second-hand house
- liquidity risk vs price risk
- policy support vs actual purchasing power

## AI And Industry Mapping

For AI and industrial-chain questions:

- L0: strategic need, compute bottleneck, energy, talent, external restrictions.
- L1: industrial policy, procurement, subsidies, standards, security requirements.
- L6: financing, capex funding, government procurement, cloud spend.
- L8: orders, chips, servers, optical modules, IDC power, software adoption, margins.
- L9: subsidy fraud, procurement distortion, overcapacity, receivables, sanctions, data/security enforcement.
- L7: valuation, crowded trades, earnings revision, theme fatigue.

Prefer financial statement translation over theme labels.

## L10 Long-Cycle Use

L10 can be useful when the user asks about long-run regime judgment, historical cycles, regional fate, or Shao Yong style framing. Keep it bounded:

- Use it after L0-L9, not before.
- State that it is analogy and framing, not empirical proof.
- Use it to enrich scenario thinking, not to replace evidence.
- Never use it for intraday, short-term, or single-stock timing.

## Forbidden Moves

- Do not answer from story-machine inputs alone.
- Do not treat media repetition as evidence.
- Do not treat official policy intent as successful implementation.
- Do not treat one CCDI case as proof of systemic regional collapse.
- Do not treat bond refinancing as proof of solvency.
- Do not treat nominal fiscal or corporate growth as real demand without checking quality.
- Do not treat one local document as national evidence.
- Do not use L10 to time markets.
- Do not give personalized financial advice or promise return.
- Do not use stale facts when the answer requires current facts.

## Example Prompts

### 成都债务风险大吗？

Expected handling:

- Define whether the user means municipal solvency, LGFV bond risk, real estate drag, bank exposure, or personal property risk.
- L0: population, industry, land finance, fiscal base, urbanization.
- L1: debt-resolution policy and local fiscal arrangements.
- L6: fiscal revenue, land revenue, transfer payments, LGFV maturities, refinancing.
- L9: audit, CCDI, arrears, court, bond disclosure signals.
- L7: local housing prices, rents, bond spreads.
- Conclusion: classify risk by asset type and time horizon.

### 现在能买新房吗？

Expected handling:

- Clarify if owner-occupied or investment, city, district, budget, and time horizon if needed.
- If proceeding without details, give scenario-based answer.
- L0: demographics, income, inventory, household balance sheets.
- L1: mortgage and purchase policy.
- L6/L8: mortgage credit, developer cash flow, completion risk, second-hand liquidity.
- L9: project delivery, developer debt, local fiscal stress.
- L7: rent yield, second-hand price, transaction volume.
- Conclusion: usually separate "can buy for durable self-use at conservative leverage" from "avoid speculative investment unless evidence is exceptional."

### 中纪委信息对资产配置有什么用？

Expected handling:

- Explain CCDI as an L9 crack-signal source, not a direct buy/sell signal.
- Map investigations to affected balance sheets, project chains, sectors, regions, and policy enforcement direction.
- Cross-check with audit, fiscal, bond, procurement, court, and company filings.
- Use findings to adjust risk premium, avoid false policy stories, and identify where cash flow is weaker than the narrative.
